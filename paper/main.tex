\title{Decide on Arrival: Placement Timing Beats Placement Prediction on Production KV Traces}
\subtitle{Why split-instant evaluation understates what placement can do, and what changes when the
decision moves}

\author{STOA Project}
\affiliation{%
  \institution{Semyung University}
  \city{Jecheon}
  \country{Republic of Korea}}
\email{suanlee@semyung.ac.kr}

\begin{abstract}
LLM agents increasingly depend on external memory, and the decision that governs it---for each memory
item, in what \emph{representation}, on which \emph{storage tier}, and \emph{when} to (re)materialize
it---is today split across three communities that each optimize one axis and fix the others. We give a
single budget-constrained MDP over representation~$\times$~tier~$\times$~timing with one serve-time
$(L,C,B)$ knob, and then ask the empirical question it invites on two production KV-trace sources: what
does a placement policy have to get right?

Our answer is that it has to decide \emph{early}, not \emph{well}. Placement studies of this kind---ours
included, for three revisions---fix a split instant, build features from the prefix, and place the
blocks that already exist. On these traces 45--46\% of blocks have no pre-split access at all, and they
carry \textbf{97\%} of a hindsight oracle's advantage: the protocol makes almost all of the benefit
unreachable, and any policy measured inside it looks flat however well it ranks. Moving the decision to
each block's \emph{arrival} raises the reachable share from 3\% to \textbf{84\%} on one trace and 2\% to
\textbf{19\%} on the other. In that regime, first-come-first-served admission with a fixed action
captures \textbf{87\%} of what is reachable on the first trace but only \textbf{50\%} on the second, and
a learned arrival-time ranker (AUC 0.57, 0.64) captures less than FCFS on both. Timing is what recovers
the benefit; whether a trivial policy then captures it varies by workload, and the arrival order itself
is worth 14 points on one of the two traces.

Reaction is competitive for the same reason. Rules that forecast nothing---LRU, LFU, a fill-once
cache---reach 75--99\% of Belady's hit rate on one trace and 40--94\% on the other, and the whole
adaptive-replacement family (ARC, LeCaR, CACHEUS) beats the best of them by at most 4.6 points; all four
adapt on a ghost hit, and 76--78\% of blocks are never reused, so the signal is scarce by construction.

The protocol finding is not specific to us. Split-instant evaluation is the standard way this decision is
studied, and on any workload where a large share of items arrive after the split it will understate what
placement can do --- by a factor of 27 on one of our traces and 8 on the other. We give the diagnostic that detects it in one line (compare
the oracle's advantage against the share of it a causal policy could reach) and the correction that
removes it. Our own three retractions are reported as evidence for how invisible the failure is: each
produced a plausible aggregate, and none was caught by any check that did not compare a reported
quantity against what it was arithmetically able to be.
\end{abstract}
