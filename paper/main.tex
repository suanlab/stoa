\begin{abstract}
KV-cache placement across GPU, host, remote and storage tiers is evaluated by replaying traces, and the
choices such an evaluation fixes silently decide what it reports. On two production Mooncake traces,
92.8\% and 95.0\% of the blocks seen before the evaluation split are never read again; with the blocks not
yet created, they give a placement policy no evidence of future use, and how an evaluation orders them is
a tie rule. Under the rule earlier versions of this paper used, a clairvoyant split-time reference reaches
20.4\% and 15.3\% of a hindsight oracle's advantage, suggesting most of it is out of a split-time policy's
reach; with the dead blocks parked behind new ones the same reference reaches 77.8\% and 76.2\%. Knowing
which seen blocks are dead is worth as much as knowing their whole future, and a parameter-free rule that
parks blocks seen once behind new ones recovers 39.2\% and 43.3\% (47.0\% and 51.7\% with an estimated
prior, which three of four Alibaba serving traces support). Online, a multi-tier LRU captures 84.5\% and
87.7\% when moving a block is free, but at the move prices we measured on an A100 host, 0.75--1.87 times one
read plus one write, and under five ways of billing a move, none of the replayed policies that move blocks
beats a static frequency heuristic. Reactive replacement, S3-FIFO, SIEVE and W-TinyLFU included, stays
within a few points of plain rules. An artifact regenerates every number on CPU, and we catalogue the
twenty-eight defects, four of them retracted claims, that produced plausible numbers on the way.
\end{abstract}
