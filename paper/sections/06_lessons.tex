Three lessons generalize beyond KV caches, one per convention. \emph{State the order of blocks without
evidence}: any reference or policy that gives two blocks the same belief places them by a tie rule, and when
most blocks have no evidence and the fast tier holds the live ones, the rule is the result; LRU-K named this case in 1993 and left it to a
``subsidiary policy''~\cite{oneil1993lruk}. \emph{Count hits the way the engine serves them}: a simulator
that scores a resident block behind a miss reports what no engine delivers, and a policy that fragments
prefixes can win on that count and lose on the engine's. \emph{Price what a policy does by the path it
takes}: a store or a move costs a request what its write path makes it cost, from nothing on an asynchronous
connector to many times its transfer time on a blocking one under load, so the price has to be measured in
the stack or bounded, not assumed. Behind all three sits a fourth: treat every convenience choice as a
variable; the split, trace length, tier capacity, action space, magnitude scale and billing of creations each moved
a headline by tens of points here. The harness ships with these as sweeps, and with a checker that re-derives every number in
this paper from its artifact and fails on drift.
