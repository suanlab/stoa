\section{What the Measurement Took}
\label{sec:lessons}
Twenty-nine defects produced plausible numbers that matched their artifacts on the way here
(\Cref{tab:defects}), and four of them were claims we had to retract: the three in the row of that name
and the tie rule. Most were found by re-verification passes after the
paper passed every check it defined for itself; two by an adversarial review and five by an audit, both
run with AI agents (see the Acknowledgments), one by a later review that asked whether our reference
was an upper bound, and the last by a reviewer's question about action spaces. The artifact's lab notebook records each with its fix.

Three lessons generalize beyond KV caches. \emph{State the order of blocks without evidence}: any
reference or policy that gives two blocks the same belief places them by a tie rule, and when most
blocks have no evidence the rule is the result; LRU-K named this case in 1993 and left it to a
``subsidiary policy''~\cite{oneil1993lruk}. \emph{Treat every convenience choice as a variable}: the
split, trace length, action space, tie rule, magnitude scale and billing of creations each moved a
headline by tens of points here. \emph{Price what a policy does, not only what it serves}: a policy that
moves blocks is only as good as the move is cheap, and the price has to be measured or bounded rather
than assumed. The harness ships with these as sweeps, and with a checker that re-derives every number in
this paper from its artifact and fails on drift.
