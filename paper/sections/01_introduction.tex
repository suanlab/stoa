\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
LLM serving systems now keep the attention state of past requests, the KV cache, across a hierarchy of
GPU memory, host memory, remote memory and storage, so that a later request sharing a prefix reads it
instead of recomputing it~\cite{qin2024mooncake,gao2024cachedattention,F5_liu2025lmcache,zhang2026polarkv}.
Which tier holds which block is a buffer-management decision~\cite{pan2025dbperspective}, and like every
such decision it is evaluated by replaying a trace: let a policy observe part of it, place blocks, and bill
the rest against a hindsight reference. This paper is about what that evaluation has to fix, because on
production KV traces the choices it makes silently decide what it reports.

Most blocks such an evaluation shows a policy are already dead. On two production Mooncake traces,
\textbf{92.8\% and 95.0\%} of the blocks seen before the midpoint are never read again; the few that are
fit in the fastest tier, and the rest of the reuse falls on blocks the policy has not seen. Dead and unseen
blocks give a split-time policy no evidence of future use, and a reference that gives both the same belief
orders them by a tie rule, the case LRU-K left to a ``subsidiary policy''~\cite{oneil1993lruk}.
Earlier versions of this paper broke that tie by item order and reported that most of a hindsight oracle's
advantage is out of a split-time policy's reach: a clairvoyant split-time reference reached \textbf{20.4\%
and 15.3\%} of it. Parking the dead blocks behind the new ones costs them nothing, and the same reference
reaches \textbf{77.8\% and 76.2\%} (\Cref{sec:ties}). Neither is an upper bound; the tie rule was the
headline.

Separating what the oracle knows shows what placement has to get right. Knowing \emph{which} seen blocks
are dead is worth exactly as much as knowing every seen block's future (\Cref{sec:ladder}). The prefix
approximates it: a block seen once is dead with probability 0.95 and 0.97, and a rule that parks seen-once
blocks behind new ones, with no parameters, captures 39.2\% and 43.3\% of the advantage; an estimated prior
for new blocks captures 47.0\% and 51.7\%, and on three of Alibaba's four serving traces it points the
same way (\Cref{sec:arrival}). A better ranker of the seen blocks, by contrast, gains at most 8.2\% and
7.4\% (\Cref{sec:capacity}).

Static analysis assumes that a block, once placed, stays. Replayed online, plain recency does the same job
better: a multi-tier LRU captures 84.5\% and 87.7\% when moving a block is free, more than any arrival-time
policy (\Cref{sec:online}). Whether anything that moves blocks pays is set by the price of a move, which
KV-cache studies usually assume away (\Cref{tab:protocols}). We measured it on an A100 host: a move between
GPU and host memory costs 0.75--1.87 times one read plus one write, at least 10 times what the arrival-time
gain could afford, and under every way of billing a move we tried, none of the replayed policies that move
blocks beats a static frequency heuristic. Reactive replacement, finally, stays within a few points of
plain rules: the adaptive family (ARC, LeCaR, CACHEUS) beats the best plain rule by at most 4.6 points of
Belady, a learned LRB stays below ARC, and S3-FIFO, SIEVE and W-TinyLFU stay below the best plain rule
everywhere, because most blocks never produce the second access these designs act on (\Cref{sec:reaction}).

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{(C1) What an evaluation has to fix.} The decision instant, information set, billing of
  creations, references, tie rule for blocks without evidence, and the price and ledger of a move, each
  stated and swept (\Cref{sec:defs}); none of the KV-cache studies we read states the tie rule
  (\Cref{tab:protocols}).
  \item \textbf{(C2) Findings on production traces}, replicated on Alibaba's traces where the data allow:
  the tie rule moves a clairvoyant reference by 48--63 points; deadness, not ranking, is what placement has
  to get right, and a parameter-free prefix rule recovers about half of it; at measured move prices
  nothing that moves blocks pays; modern one-hit-wonder policies do not beat plain rules.
  \item \textbf{(C3) An artifact and a record.} Every number regenerates on CPU from public traces and a
  checker fails on drift; the twenty-eight defects that each produced a plausible number on the way here,
  four of them retracted claims, are catalogued with the control that catches each (\Cref{sec:lessons}).
\end{itemize}
